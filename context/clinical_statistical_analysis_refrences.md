following statistical approaches and tools are used for different types of cancer data and groups:
Incidence and Mortality Analysis
SEER*Stat Rate Session: Used for calculating basic cancer incidence and mortality rates
.
Age-Adjusted Rates: Applied when comparing populations to reduce the confounding effect of age by using a weighted average based on a standard population
.
Gamma Confidence Intervals: Used for reporting age-adjusted rates, particularly when dealing with rare cancers or small populations
.
Multiple Primary-Standardized Incidence Ratios (MP-SIR): Used to test hypotheses regarding the links between two different cancers by comparing a specific cohort's subsequent cancer experience to the general population
.
Incidence-Based Mortality (IBM): Employed to partition mortality by onset variables—such as age, stage of disease, or histology—to determine their contribution to death
.
Trend Analysis
Joinpoint Regression Program: Used to analyze trends over time in incidence and mortality rates, identifying the specific points where those trends change
.
Delay Model: Utilized to adjust current case counts to account for anticipated future corrections or reporting delays, allowing for more precise current trend determination
.
Risk and Prevalence Analysis
DevCan: Used to calculate the lifetime risk or probability of a person being diagnosed with or dying from cancer
.
SEER*Stat Prevalence Session: Estimates "limited-duration prevalence," which counts cases alive on a certain date who were diagnosed within a specific past timeframe (e.g., the last 20 years)
.
Complete Prevalence (ComPrev): Used to estimate the total number of people alive who have ever had a diagnosis, regardless of how long ago it occurred
.
Projected Prevalence (ProjPrev): Employed to apply prevalence statistics from one population (like SEER) to a different target population
.
