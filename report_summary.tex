\section{Methods}
\subsection{Data processing}
I processed the clinical metadata of the PBTA RNA study which includes two tables: patient-level and sample-level tables
with 2870 and 4312 samples, respectively. I merged the two tables on the patient id, which resulted in 4074 samples
(2701 unique patients) with a known CG. To analyze the data I converted the OS and EFS status values into labels and event
flags, merged missing RACE and ETHINICY values into "Unknown", mapped the cancer PREDISPOSITION into 
"No predisposition"/"Unknown", and merged blank or "To be classified" molecular subtypes into "Unclassified". 
For the AGE distribution I detected outliers with the Tukey IQR rule (AGE > Q3 + 1.5*IQR = 27.125 yr), which flagged 
62 patients.

\subsubsection{Statistic analysis}
To evalute the data statisticaly, I computed the \% of missing values in each column to identify noisy variables. 
To compare CGs, I tested every CG with n >= 20 samples (excluding 33 CGs): SEX was tested against a 50:50 baseline with a 
binomial test, while PREDISPOSITION and RACE compositions were tested with Chi-squared tests 
(Fisher exact when the expected counts were < 5), reporting Cramer's V as the effect size. 
For the numeric variables AGE, TUMOR\_FRACTION (TF), and TUMOR\_PLIDY (TP) i used a Kruskal-Wallis test followed by per-group Mann-Whitney tests
(each CG vs all the others). i corrected the p-values within each family of tests using the Benjamini-Hochberg FDR
method and considered q < 0.05 as significant, always reporting the raw p next to the adjusted q. 
Finaly, i computed spearman (and pearson) correlations between AGE, TF and TP, and ordered the CGs by similar
race composition using hierarchical clustering. All the processing and statistics were implemented in python
(pandas, scipy, statsmodels) as executed notebooks.

\subsubsection{Survival analysis}
To test whether the previously analyzed variables affect survival, I used time-to-event
analyses on the OS and EFS endpoints. I plotted Kaplan--Meier (KM) curves per CG ($n \geq 20$) and 
each group's survival was compared against all others combined
with log-rank tests (multiple-testing correction as above). Each variable was then tested
against survival status (Mann--Whitney $U$ for AGE, TF, and TP; Chi-squared for SEX and
predisposition), and numeric variables were dichotomized at their median to test KM
separation with log-rank tests. Finally, I fitted a multivariate Cox proportional-hazards
model with AGE, SEX, TUMOR\_FRACTION, and TUMOR\_PLOIDY as covariates, stratified by cancer
group, on complete cases, reporting Wald p-values, hazard ratios with 95\% confidence
intervals, and Schoenfeld residuals to check the proportional-hazards assumption. Analyses
were implemented in Python (scipy, lifelines) as executed notebooks.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Meta data and clinical analysis}
To begin with i conducut a simple visualization and summarization of the clinical and demogrpahic data including statstics and 
histograms. I noticed that event-free surival (EFS) and overall survival (OS) months variables has ~28\% Null values (Figure 1A). 
In total 65.3\% has a living status via the OS variabel, whereas for the EFS variable, half the patients exhibit no event 
and the other half splitted between different events (Figure 1B, C). This demonstrate the value of investigating the 
EFS rather than OS where EFS provides additional information ~cite{}. Furthermore, the "AGE" (age of diagnosis) right skewed
historgram suggests outliers or misleading data points as this is a kids dataset (Figure 1D). A further inspection revealed
that most of this data points (53/62) belong to oligodendrogliomas paitens (Figure 1E). A quick web search suggested
that this cancer is an adult cancer, and therefor removing these data points for subseqeunt analysis should be consider.

Investigating the samples dataset demonstrated ~20-40\% Null values in Tumor fraction (TF) and ploidy (TP), RNA selection, and 
matching id samples (Figure 2A). Among the 55 cancer groups Low-grade glioma (LGG) has most cases (862). 
3 groups has 300-500, 7 groups has 70-200 cases, and 45 groups has less then 70 cases (Figure 2B). The molecular
subtype information aligns well with the cancer group, despite 13 molecular subtypes belonging to 2 or more disticnt CGs
(Figure 2C). This suggests eather wrong labeling of the sub molecular type or some sub type belonging 
to multiple CGs.

\subsection{Cross datasets analysis}
Next, to reveal diffrences between cancer groups, I evaluated whether certain variales are enrichet among differnet cancer groups.
To do so, I evaluated the categorical variables "SEX", "predisposition", and "race" using Binomial, Chi-squared and Fisher extact
tests. For the numeric variables "AGE", "TF", and "TP" i used Kruskal-Wallis and per-group Mann-Whitney tests between CGs. 

I found that for 6 CGs the male to female disterbution is sigfnicaly different (Figure 3A) compared to 50:50 baseline.
Furthermore, among patients with predispositions (~9\%), 5 CGs have different prediposition composition (Figure 3B).

These findings suggests that sex, race and predisposition could co-occur with different CGs. Lastly as demonstrated in

A further inspection on these CGs could reveal the connection. Next, I demonstarted that 14, 6, and 4 CGs are significally
diffiriante by AGE, TF, and TP variables (Figure 3D, E, F). Investigating their pearson/spearman correlations (Figure 3G, H)
yielded significant p values (5/6) with no observed trends possibly due to high sample amount.
